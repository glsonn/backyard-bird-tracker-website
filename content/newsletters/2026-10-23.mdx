People use calendars. Birds don’t.

Obvious, right?

At least, they don’t use the paper or electronic kind that we do. So when you think you’ve seen the last of a species for the season or the year because it seems right according to the calendar, you shouldn’t be surprised when you see them again anyway.

But I was.

## This Week in the Backyard

My robins, who had seemingly gone into hiding for the winter, suddenly reappeared in force this past week. Not just the 2 or 3 or 4 I’d been seeing in spring and summer, but up to a dozen or so all at once.

Now, they didn’t all land in my yard or on the birdbath at the same time. They’re too argumentative for that. I think the most I could actually see at once was maybe 5 or 6, but I knew there were at least twice that many within a stone’s throw.

And they were really kinda nasty. One even chased a Mourning Dove off the box feeder!

## What to Watch For This Week

Look for birds returning unexpectedly - like my robins.

Maybe your robins are doing the same thing…?

## Backyard Moment

We had temperatures in the 80s this past week. That feels really hot when you’re already used to the fall temps of the 70s and 60s here in Wisconsin.

I think it was some time during this week that it really did first feel like fall. The leaves are turning and falling. I think I mowed leaves as much as grass the last time I cut the lawn.

## Journal Prompt

What changed? Revisit something that you noticed (and maybe wrote about) a few weeks ago. How is it different now?

## From the Backyard Bird Tracker

I’m working on adding to the BBT app all the 1000+ species from the official American Bird Association list of North American birds. They won’t all have photos attached to them like my original ~70 birds do, but at least they’ll be readily [available in the app](https://www.backyardbirdtracker.com/app) for you when you record your sightings.

It’ll probably take me a few weeks to get them all added, but don’t be surprised to see 100 more there from time to time.

## Enjoy the Little Changes

Before winter really sets in (and I have to get most of my carrots out of the ground), notice the little changes that are happening from day to day and from week to week.

How?

Hopefully you’re one of those who’s keeping a journal (see above) or using the app (also see above) and can easily look back to see what’s different.

If you haven’t started doing either yet, there’s no time like the present.

It’s the time of year when it often makes more sense to have your eyes focused on the ground than on a feeder or a tree or the sky.

This week we’ll explore this idea in some detail because the ground can be quite interesting in November.

## This Week in the “Backyard”

In my own backyard, the Dark-eyed Juncos have been the most noticeable addition at ground level. I’ve mentioned these guys before, but I’ve really noticed over the past few weeks how they really don’t care for the tube feeders at all.

They much prefer to forage for what other birds have knocked out of the tubes or off of the box. Once in a while, a single junco will perch on the box feeder for a while. I don’t think I’ve ever seen two of them there at the same time though - certainly not like the handful of sparrows or finches who will often congregate there.

## What to Watch For This Week

The obvious thing to watch for this week is this: Who’s feeding on the ground below your feeder(s) or tree(s) or bush(es).

## Backyard Moment

There’s often one or two Mourning Doves on my box feeder, but because of their size, I’ll often see a bunch of them on the ground. In fact, just today I saw 9 of them all in the same area.

The Northern Cardinals that visit my backyard usually head for the box feeder too, but recently they have also been targeting the ground instead.

## Journal Prompt

Take a minute to write about what you see below your feeder (or tree or bush) this week. Any surprises there?

## From the Backyard Bird Tracker

So bird feeders aren’t the be-all and end-all that most people probably think they are. We now know that the ground itself is a natural place to find our birds.

While you’re keeping track of your sightings - in [Backyard Bird Tracker](https://www.backyardbirdtracker.com/) or elsewhere - maybe add a note about where the birds were.

Were they on the feeder? Were they on the ground below the feeder? Were they out in the open away from the feeder?

## Looking Down for Something New

Sometimes looking up is the best direction to look while birding. After all, birds do fly a lot. (And sometimes a Cooper’s Hawk will perch on the neighbor’s rooftop. Yes, mine did this week.)

But just as often it pays to look down at the ground among the grass (and weeds) and leaves and trunks and flowers and vegetables to see what’s new down there.

I had come up with the subject line for this newsletter several weeks ago. Little did I know then how appropriate it would feel today.

It’s mid-afternoon as I write this. So far today I’ve seen a grand total of 5 birds.

Not 5 species. Five birds! (Only 3 species.)

That’s waaay below my normal count. Later in this newsletter, I’ll talk about this in more detail.

## This Week in the “Backyard”

I put “backyard” in quotation marks just above because I’m going “outside the yard” this time.

We got to spend a couple of days in Door County this past week. (For those who are unfamiliar with the area, Door County is the “thumb” peninsula of Wisconsin that helps form Green Bay - the watery bit.)

We’ve tried twice on past visits to hike the Sentinel Trail in Peninsula State Park, one of Wisconsin’s more popular parks. The first time, we got rained out. (The forecast lied to me.) The second time, they were resurfacing the parking lots, and we couldn’t find a spot to park - at least, not one that made sense.

This time, everything from our parking spot to the weather was perfect. Mission accomplished.
And the icing on the cake was that we even saw several birds. (This is actually rather uncommon for us on our hikes. I think we’re too noisy.)

We spotted a Hairy Woodpecker, a Pileated Woodpecker, and probably about two dozen Dark-eyed Juncos.

A more than good time was had by all.

## What to Watch For This Week

As you sight birds this week, pay attention to which ones are sticking around and which ones have apparently left for the season.

## Backyard Moment

As I mentioned above, I’ve seen 5 birds so far today. (I expect that total will rise before sunset.) I’ve seen 2 juncos several times. I have to assume it’s the same pair, but I could be wrong. I’ve seen 2 Mourning Doves and 1 American Robin.

My biggest question is: What happened to the half dozen House Finches and House Sparrows that I normally see?

It could be the temperature. It’s not going to hit 60 today, which makes this one of the coolest days so far this fall.

Maybe there’s a predator in the area - a cat or a hawk - that’s keeping them away. But that would likely keep those 5 sighted birds away too.

Could they all simply have “flown the coop” (to use a somewhat related phrase)?

In the end, I really don’t know. Any ideas?

## From the Backyard Bird Tracker

Sometimes it feels like only the uncommonly-seen species are worth keeping track of. That House Sparrow - probably one you’ve seen a few dozen times - just doesn’t seem that special.

But he is.

Ordinary sightings are worth keeping just as much as any other.

Why?

Because, when you look back at your records a month, a year, or 5 years from now, you’ll be able to see patterns that you otherwise would have missed.

That might not feel like a big deal when you’re recording the sighting, but you’ll notice it later, especially if you add a short comment to it.

[Backyard Bird Tracker](https://www.backyardbirdtracker.com/) is perfect for just this situation. If you’ve hesitated to try it out, now would be a great time to give it a whirl. It’s still free.

## 5 + 1 = 6

Math Word Problem: Gary saw 5 birds in the morning. Later that day, he saw one more bird. How many birds did Gary see all together?

Yes, math is hard, but I think we can all agree that, since I’ve now seen a Blue Jay in my backyard while writing this, I can add 1 to my daily total (in Backyard Bird Tracker) to the previous 5 for a grand total of 6 sightings today.

Oh boy, oh boy!

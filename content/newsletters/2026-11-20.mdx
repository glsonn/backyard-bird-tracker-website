Winter is technically about a month away, but as I write this in Wisconsin, the temps are in the 30s and the wind chill is in the 20s, so it sure feels like winter already.

Before the winter crowd of birds appears in your backyard, there may be some changes you want to make. Let’s take a look at some of the possibilities.

## This Week in the Backyard

In preparation for the new species that (hopefully) will soon show up in the backyard, you might want to consider changing the types of food you offer. Do the birds that usually winter in your area eat the same types of seeds that your summer birds do?

If you have a bird bath in the yard, is it the heated type that you’ll keep offering all winter? Mine isn’t heated, but I plan to keep it out there at least until the snow flies and maybe until the drifts get too deep to maintain it properly. We’ll see. I may change my mind on that depending on when that first big storm is supposed to hit.

What about the types of feeders you’ll keep filling? I’ve always kept my open box feeder stocked with seed because it’s the easiest to refill and to remove the snow and ice from throughout the season. Tube feeders are more problematic. I may try keeping one out there this year.

And then I’ve got that new camera feeder to consider. I’ll probably leave it out this first year to see if it can handle the weather.

## What to Watch For This Week

If you haven’t seen any Dark-eyed Juncos yet, keep an eye out for the little cuties. I’ve seen them for a while already, but not for the past couple of days. Hm.

## Backyard Moment

I’m not sure if it’s my imagination or if my birds are showing up later in the day (if at all) as the weather grows colder.

Today, for example, I’ve only seen 3 Mourning Doves so far. I’m pretty sure that normally by this time I’ve sighted several species and a dozen or more individuals.

What’s it like in your area?

## Journal Prompt

What’s your first winter visitor likely to be? When will he show up? Make a note of him (or them) when he arrives.

## Regulars and Newcomers: Notice Them All

It can be exciting to see a newcomer - like a seasonal winter bird - for the first time. And you will see one if you keep paying attention.

But don’t ignore your regulars. How are their habits changing? Are they (like mine) arriving at a different time of day? Are they sticking around for longer or shorter periods of time? Is nothing changing at all?

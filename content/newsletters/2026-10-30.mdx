I had never seen a Cooper’s Hawk before this year. Now I’ve seen one multiple times.

Is he becoming a regular?

Read on for more of my thoughts on the topic.

## This Week in the Backyard

The leaves on our maple - the only tree in our small backyard - are still falling. Gaps are appearing that let me see birds in places I hadn’t been able to spot them all summer.

It’s getting harder and harder for even the little chickadee to hide.

On the flipside, the Dark-eyed Juncos don’t seem to mind the openness. They do prefer to feed on the ground though, as long as that area is still visible.

## What to Watch For This Week

If you have trees nearby, start looking up more than you have been. See which birds you can now spot among the few leaves that are left.

## And Now…Cooper’s Hawk

A few weeks ago, I showed you my [Cooper’s Hawk capturing a robin](https://www.backyardbirdtracker.com/newsletter/2026-08-14). Since then, I’ve seen what I assume is the same bird two more times.

The first of these two sightings was my “near miss”. We had just stepped out the back door when my wife said, “What’s that big bird?”

I would have just kept on going without even looking in that direction. But yes, it was the Cooper’s Hawk perched on a low wall, obviously hoping for lunch. He flew off without it.

Then just the other day, he swooped into the backyard again. Birds scattered. None were caught. The hawk took off and sat for a few seconds on the peak of the neighbor’s roof before flying away.

Again, no lunch.

My wife was glad no birds were caught, but I noted that the hawk was going hungry. She said she’d rather have him get his meals elsewhere.

Fair enough.

So is he becoming a regular visitor to our backyard? Three times in three months qualifies for that distinction in my book.

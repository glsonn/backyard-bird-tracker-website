Have you noticed? Fall migration is still happening. Actually, they say it goes on from 4 to 6 months!

Depending on where you live, you might be experiencing it from now till Christmas.

## This Week in the Backyard

I’ve noticed that there seems to be more activity than usual in our maple tree.

It’s hard to explain, because I’m still seeing all the birds at the feeders and on the ground, but there’s something going on up above too.

It’s not nest building, of course. It’s not gathering food. (It’s a maple.) It’s just…activity.

## What to Watch For This Week

Watch the places between your feeder and the trees.

If you have a wood nearby - or even just one main tree like I have - see if your birds are spending more time in the shadows than in the open.

They might be harder to spot when sitting still, but really…how long do they ever sit still?

## Backyard Moment

You may remember that I’ve said I haven’t seen a robin for quite some time. (Per BBT, August 23.)

However, I’m pretty sure I’ve heard them on walks to the store recently.

So, like I suspected, they’re still around. It’s just that, for whatever reason(s), they don’t show up in the backyard anymore.

## Journal Prompt

Where were they?

When you jot down (or tap the app to make) your next recording of a sighting, include the location.

If you’re using the app, you’ll probably find that your device soon anticipates what you want. For example, in BBT, I only have to type a “b” to get my phone to suggest “backyard”.

Isn’t tech wonderful…sometimes?

## From the Backyard Bird Tracker

Recording the location of your sightings makes looking back at them later much more fun and interesting.

If you just added an American Crow 82 days ago, that’s okay. But if that same sighting also says, “Springfield”, so much the better…since you live in Rockford.

Jogs your memory, doesn’t it? Now you can picture exactly where that crow was sitting.

## Noticing without Identifying

As you’re looking out your window or driving down the road, don’t feel like you have to identify every bird you see.

Just noticing a flock of geese (which are really hard to count when the V is huge) is enough.

Watching that Red-winged Blackbird attacking the Red-tailed Hawk is enough.

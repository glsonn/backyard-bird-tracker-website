Ah, the Midwest. Land of cornfields, cheese curds, and—believe it or not—some of the best birdwatching spots in the country.

If you think the Midwest is just a flyover region, well, you may be right. But that’s exactly why it’s a paradise for bird lovers! Birds love flying over, stopping for snacks, and showing off their feathers in some truly stunning natural locations.

So grab your binoculars, throw on that well-loved birding vest, and let’s explore some of the best places to catch sight of our fine feathered friends.

## 1. Horicon Marsh, Wisconsin

If Wisconsin is known for cheese, Horicon Marsh is known for geese. This sprawling wetland is one of the largest freshwater marshes in the country and a major pit stop for migratory birds.

Horicon is at the lower left (southwest) corner of the map below.

<img
  src="/images/articles/horicon-map.png"
  alt="Map of the location of the Horicon Marsh"
/>

Come fall, it’s like a goose convention—honking, flapping, and generally ignoring all traffic laws. But beyond geese, you’ll find egrets, herons, and even the elusive bittern if you’re lucky. Just be prepared to leave with a little mud on your boots.

## 2. Magee Marsh, Ohio

Ever wanted to feel like you’re in the middle of a birding rock concert? Welcome to Magee Marsh, where warblers are the headliners every spring.

Magee Marsh is at the upper left (northwest) corner of the map below.

<img
  src="/images/articles/magee-marsh-map.png"
  alt="Map of the location of the Magee Marsh"
/>

This spot along Lake Erie is famous for its “warbler fallout,” a phenomenon where thousands of brightly colored songbirds take a breather before heading north. It’s basically a birdwatcher’s Super Bowl, complete with telephoto lenses and whispered exclamations of, “Oh my gosh, a Blackburnian!”

## 3. Neal Smith National Wildlife Refuge, Iowa

Prairie birdwatching, anyone? Visiting the Neal Smith National Wildlife Refuge is like stepping back in time, with rolling grasslands and a bison herd thrown in for good measure.

The Neal Smith NWR is just east of Des Moines.

<img
  src="/images/articles/neal-smith-nwr-map.jpg"
  alt="Map of the location of the Neal Smith NWR"
/>

Grasshopper sparrows, bobolinks, and meadowlarks fill the air with their songs, while hawks and eagles patrol from above. If you’re lucky, you might even see a bison giving you side-eye as you scan the skies.

## 4. Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest, Wisconsin

If you like your birdwatching with a side of deep woods adventure, this huge northern Wisconsin gem is the place for you. Warblers, owls, and loons make their homes here, and if you’re up for a challenge, try spotting the elusive spruce grouse.

The Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest is about as remote as you can get in Wisconsin.

<img
  src="/images/articles/chequamegon-nicolet-national-forest.jpg"
  alt="Map of the location of the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest"
/>

Just be prepared for mosquitoes—because nothing says “wilderness experience” like an airborne army of tiny, bloodthirsty fiends.

## 5. Sax-Zim Bog, Minnesota

Yes, it’s called a bog, but don’t let that deter you. Sax-Zim Bog is legendary among birdwatchers, especially in winter when great gray owls, boreal chickadees, and even snowy owls make an appearance.

Sax-Zim Bog is farther north than all of Wisconsin.

<img
  src="/images/articles/sax-zim-bog-map.png"
  alt="Map of the location of the Sax-Zim Bog"
/>

It’s cold, it’s remote, and it’s totally worth it. Just remember: in Minnesota (if you’re not from the Midwest), “cold” means “colder than you think is humanly possible.” Pack accordingly.

## 6. Shawnee National Forest, Illinois

Think Illinois is just Chicago and corn? Think again. Shawnee National Forest in the southern part of the state is a lush oasis filled with woodlands, waterfalls, and enough bird species to keep you entertained for days.

The Shawnee National Forest extends from the Mississippi on the west to the Ohio on the east.

<img
  src="/images/articles/shawnee-nf-map.jpg"
  alt="Map of the location of the Shawnee National Forest"
/>

Prothonotary warblers, pileated woodpeckers, and bald eagles all call this place home. Plus, the scenery is so gorgeous, you might forget you came for the birds in the first place.

## 7. Loess Bluffs National Wildlife Refuge, Missouri

Formerly known as Squaw Creek, Loess Bluffs is a top-tier birding spot, especially in the fall when migrating snow geese and trumpeter swans arrive in force. Think thousands of birds, all squawking and flapping like an avian traffic jam. It’s loud, it’s chaotic, and it’s absolutely mesmerizing.

Loess Bluffs is in northwestern Missouri just east of the Missouri River.

<img
  src="/images/articles/loess-bluffs-national-wildlife-refuge.png"
  alt="Map of the location of the Loess Bluffs NWR"
/>

## Final Thoughts

The Midwest might not have the flashy mountains or tropical beaches of other regions, but when it comes to birdwatching, it more than holds its own. Whether you love warblers, waterfowl, or the challenge of spotting an owl in the middle of a blizzard, there’s a Midwest birding spot with your name on it.

So get out there, enjoy the birds, and don’t forget to bring snacks (for birds and humans) because if there’s one thing birders and birds have in common, it’s a love of good food.
